Key facts

The rules, in brief.

From official UAE sources, last reviewed October 2026. Rules change, so we confirm what applies to your business before acting.

Legal basis
Ministerial Decisions No. 243 and 244 of 2025, amended by Ministerial Decision No. 66 of 2026. Ministry of Finance: e-invoicing amendments (2026) (opens in a new tab)
Revenue of AED 50 million or more
Deadline to appoint an Accredited Service Provider: 30 October 2026 (if it has passed, appoint one now). Implement by 1 January 2027. Ministry of Finance: e-invoicing amendments (2026) (opens in a new tab)
Revenue below AED 50 million
Appoint an Accredited Service Provider by 31 March 2027 and implement by 1 July 2027. Ministry of Finance: UAE e-invoicing (opens in a new tab)
Voluntary adoption
Businesses may implement e-invoicing voluntarily from 1 July 2026. Business-to-consumer invoices are excluded for now. FTA: UAE e-invoicing (opens in a new tab)

Questions

Common questions, answered.

When does e-invoicing become mandatory in the UAE?

On 1 January 2027 for businesses with revenue of AED 50 million or more, and on 1 July 2027 for businesses below that. Each group must appoint an Accredited Service Provider first; the deadlines are 30 October 2026 and 31 March 2027 respectively.

What is an Accredited Service Provider (ASP)?

A provider approved by the authorities to exchange and report e-invoices on your behalf. You appoint one and connect it to the system that raises your invoices.

Is a PDF invoice sent by email an e-invoice?

No. Under the UAE system an e-invoice is a structured data file exchanged through accredited providers, not a PDF or scanned document.

Do invoices to individual consumers have to be e-invoices?

Not for now. Business-to-consumer transactions are excluded from the current phase.

What should we do first?

Confirm your revenue band and deadlines, check what your accounting system can export, and shortlist providers. That assessment is our first step with every client.
